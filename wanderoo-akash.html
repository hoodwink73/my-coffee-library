03 · if the first cup is…

One change per brew.
Numbers are for the suggested recipe.

V60: sour, thin, short → grind finer, Ode 5.1 → 4.2, or pour a little slower. If it is still sour, go to 93 °C.

V60: bitter, drying → grind coarser, Ode 5.1 → 6.0.

French press: sour or thin → stir a few times just before plunging, or steep 30 seconds longer.

French press: muddy, silty → grind coarser, skim the foam, and leave the last 20 g in the press.

Harsh but not drying → too strong: add 20–30 g hot water to the cup before touching the grinder.

  4. 9:30—Rest the plunger just under the surface, pour slowly, leave the last 20 g
why this recipe
picked for
a different brewer: a full immersion press

Hoffmann's method: a long steep, a crust break, a skim, and a long settle. The plunger only works as a lid, never pressed through the bed, so the cup comes out clean and sweet with almost no silt. It is the slow one.

James Hoffmann, via LAMOSE and Volcanica's test (15 g : 225 g, about 90 °C) · grade B · grind H, the 1:15 scale and the 20 g left behind are mine

why this recipe
picked for
a different brewer: a stronger, faster press

An Indian roaster's own press guide: a short bloom, a 3-minute steep and a slow plunge. Stronger and quicker than Hoffmann's, with more body. For a medium roast the guide says stir a few times just before plunging to cut sourness.
