03 · if the first cup is…

One change per brew.
Numbers are for the suggested recipe.

Sour, thin, short → grind finer: Ode 5.1 → 4.2. You can also go hotter, 97–98 °C.

Bitter, drying → grind coarser: Ode 5.1 → 6, and make the 0:30 pour a gentle spiral.

Sour and bitter at once → fix evenness before grind: swirl the bloom, pour lower and gentler, or drop to 16 g : 249 g.

04 · three experiments, one change each

Tear one off.

EXP 01B · Coffee ad Astra

Spin length

Finish with a 2-second swirl. Next brew, a 7-second one. Time the drawdown.

expect · A heavy spin drives fines down and slows the drain. Keep the cleaner cup.

try it on · Chiroso four-pour

EXP 02B · Philocoffea

Sweet vs bright

Second pour 60 g (then 90 + 90). Next brew, 100 g (then 70 + 70).

expect · Smaller early pour = sweeter. Larger = brighter.

try it on · Chiroso four-pour

EXP 03B · SCA cupping

Taste it cooling

Sip at about 70, 60 and 45 °C, then at room temperature.

expect · Perfume hot, acidity warm, the peach as it cools.

try it on · any of the three

wildcards, if you're feeling it · Switch valve open vs closed · Flash-iced · Days off roast · Device swap: V60 ↔ Deep 27

why this recipe
matched on
washed · chiroso · colombia · light

Onyx wrote this for another high-grown washed Colombian Chiroso: a fairly strong 1:15.6, four pours and a quick drain to keep the florals clean. Chiroso is an Ethiopian landrace, so the grind is one click coarser than written for a slower bed (house estimate).

why this recipe
picked for
a different brewer, a tiny dose

Hedrick uses the narrow Deep 27 to give a small dose a deep bed, with many small pours against bypass and a coarse grind against bitterness. A single cup that leans into the peach and keeps the finish light. Times after 0:40 and the temperature are house estimates.

  2. 0:45+100 g → 150 gOpen valve. 95 °C, centre pour
  3. 1:45+70 g → 220 gClose valve. 80 °C water
  4. 2:30—Open valve, let it drain
  5. 2:55—Done
why this recipe
picked for
a far style: valve hybrid, two temperatures

Cool immersion first for sweetness, a hot open pour for the bright acidity, then a cool steep for a juicy body. It suits the blackcurrant and guava. Fukahori brewed it on a valve dripper to win the 2018 World Brewers Cup; it is moved to the Switch here, and the source is secondhand, so dial it in.

Emi Fukahori, WBrC 2018 · grade C
